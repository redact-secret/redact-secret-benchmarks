# Attribute public cases by the legacy targets where the snapshot names none, and keep floors per population

## Context

#638, part of #602, refs #607, #608 and #636. Nine legacy-stable families were also held by a floor on the new path: eight read as
`fixture-attribution` (digitalocean-token, docker-token, gitlab-token, npm-token, pulumi-access-token, shopify-token,
terraform-cloud-token, vault-token) and one as `population-separation` (sendgrid-token).

The data, on the canonical public artifact: of 5,950 cases, 251 carry neither a family nor a target. Of the
eight families' missing cases, none is a twin: they are plain controls and positives (the snapshot gives them no family) that the legacy
path scoped to the family by their declared targets (`benchmarks/fixture-detectors.json`, else the fixture's `detectors`). Twins
with a `twin_of` and no family are 22 (six that the legacy path scoped to the parent's family, the others unscoped on both sides).
The legacy floor rule attributes a fixture to the detectors in its `targets`, and counts the pooled development, regression and
policy fixtures in one denominator.

## Decision

1. **A case the snapshot attributes to no detector is attributed by the policy's fallback, in order** (`attribution.fallback` in
   `benchmarks/support/population-policy.json`, validated): first the legacy targets that the product overlay carries for the case
   (`public-axis-overlay.json` `detectors`, generated by the same content join as the axes, never hand-authored, filtered to product
   detector ids), then the detectors of the case's twin parent. What the snapshot names always wins; a case no step attributes stays
   unattributed and counted. This reproduces the legacy attribution for the cases the snapshot leaves unnamed. Public evidence classes
   and credential-evidence are untouched; this is product policy about which family a counted case belongs to.
2. **The twin-parent step is implemented and matches the legacy rule.** A twin is scoped to the family it twins. On the canonical
   corpus every twin it could attribute is already attributed by the overlay (it attributes none beyond it), so it changes no number
   today; it keeps the attribution deterministic where the overlay has no entry (a new fixture) and equals the legacy scope where it
   applies. It chains no further than the parent's own attribution.
3. **Floors stay per population: sendgrid-token is a legitimate difference.** The legacy rule pooled the family's regression fixtures
   into its control axes and cases. The no-pooling rule of #603 and `population-policy.json` forbids it: floors and fixture-profile cells
   are read from the floors-and-gates population alone, because regression fixtures were authored to pin one defect each and would inflate
   a count they were not authored to measure. The family has three control axes on the public snapshot against the four of the legacy
   pooled count, so it reads provisional on `documented.minimumControlAxes`. Giving it a fourth axis is a corpus decision for
   credential-evidence or a policy decision about the regression corpus, neither made here. It is reported as `population-separation`.
4. **Twin discrimination is not re-scored.** Attributing a cross-provider twin to its family exposes a difference the engine owns: the
   engine scopes a twin by the twin's own family, the snapshot gives a cross-provider twin none, so a finding of another known detector
   reads as flagged where the legacy twin read it as co-detected. The adapter sums the engine's per-case fields and does not recompute a
   verdict from findings (credential-eval qualification-boundary.md section 4.1), so three families (anthropic-admin01-key,
   anthropic-api01-key, elevenlabs-api-key) now read `twinFailures: 2` and are held at provisional. The difference is reported as
   `twin-scope-vocabulary` and fails closed. It needs credential-evidence to carry a family on such a twin, or credential-eval to scope a
   twin by its parent's family.

## Consequences

- The eight `fixture-attribution` families read their floors from the same cases the legacy path counted. The residual verdict
  differences are three twin-scope families and one population-separation family, each attributed.
- The overlay is regenerated when the snapshot is repinned (`npm run qualification:axis-overlay -- --snapshot <file>`).

## Rejected

- Pooling the regression corpus into the sendgrid-token floor: contradicts #603 and would let defect-pinning fixtures inflate a floor.
- Attributing by the twin parent alone, as first proposed: it fixes none of the eight families (their missing cases are not twins),
  and where the legacy path left a twin unscoped it would invent an attribution.
- Hand-listing the cases per family: it drifts and is not the legacy rule.
- Recomputing a twin's verdict from its findings in the adapter, to reach 127: it re-scores a case the engine scored, and the scope it
  needs is the engine's input to fix.
