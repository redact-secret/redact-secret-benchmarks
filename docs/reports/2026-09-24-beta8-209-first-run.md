# Beta.8 #209 first run: documented-stable hardening

Issue: [#209](https://github.com/redact-secret/redact-secret-benchmarks/issues/209).
Research: redact-secret/redact-secret#642 (azure-devops, google, notion,
anthropic), redact-secret/redact-secret#645 (datadog),
[#234](https://github.com/redact-secret/redact-secret-benchmarks/issues/234)
(confluent) and [#235](https://github.com/redact-secret/redact-secret-benchmarks/issues/235)
(heroku). Layout: `docs/specs/beta8-evidence.md`.

This report records a first measurement. It changes no support claim, and no
fixture expectation was edited after the run.

## What changed

- **Corpus `beta8-209`** (`fixtures/generated/beta8/209.mjs`): 68 fixtures,
  all targeting registry detectors. There are 28 positives, 19 independent
  controls and 21 single-property twins. Each family gets four positives on
  four new context axes. None of the pre-existing positives carried a
  `contextAxis`.
- **Contracts** (`benchmarks/lib/assessment.ts`): the seven contracts gain
  per-field provenance (`FIELDS_209`). Provider URLs, corroboration and tiers
  are unchanged. Two contracts change grammar:
  - `confluent-cloud-api-secret` adds `validate`. The last 6 characters must be
    the standard-Base64 prefix of the little-endian CRC32 of the 54 body
    characters, with the `cflt` prefix excluded. The provider publishes this
    algorithm as a snippet on its documentation page. This replaces the earlier
    claim that the checksum is "documented but not part of this contract's
    lexical pattern" and the product source's claim that it cannot be
    recomputed.
  - `heroku-api-key` accepts the documented 41-character `HRKU-<uuid>`
    generation next to the 65-character `HRKU-AA…` one. The `AA` start and the
    body alphabet are recorded as example/tool-observed (`provisional`), not
    provider-stated. The bare-UUID generation stays with
    `heroku-api-key-legacy` (owned by #207) and is recorded as a field only.
- **Classification:**
  - `matches()` now honours a contract's `validate`.
  - Azure DevOps's policy route is limited to `detector-coverage`, so the Beta.8
    positives are scored on the T1 contract.
  - The `checksum` mutation kind is new.
  - The lexical-separability check counts a pattern match only when the
    contract's `validate` accepts it.

### Findings (frozen; no detector fix, no expectation change)

1. **Confluent checksum not validated by the product.** The three
   checksum-invalid twins are flagged:
   - a changed checksum character;
   - the checksum computed with big-endian bytes;
   - the checksum computed with the prefix included.

   Their `authored.twin` assertions fail. Both peers match on shape only too.
   The URL-safe-alphabet twin is discriminated.
2. **Confluent public key ID flagged.** In the AWS Secrets Manager JSON
   positive, the product flags the documented-public API key ID in the
   `"apiKey"` field as `generic-token` (16 bytes of collateral). The key ID in
   `CONFLUENT_CLOUD_API_KEY=`, `sasl.username=` and CLI `"key"` contexts stays
   clean.
3. **Heroku 41-character generation missed.** The product finds nothing on
   either `HRKU-<uuid>` positive (env, `authorizations:create` transcript).
   gitleaks's generic rule matches the env value. trufflehog reports only the
   UUID part.
4. **Google Firebase web config (disputed).** The product finds nothing on
   `apiKey: "AIza…"` inside a `firebaseConfig` object that has `authDomain` and
   `projectId`. The product source suppresses this on purpose
   (`pipeline.rs`, redact-secret#520 B3a). It cites Firebase's statement that
   keys restricted to Firebase services need not be treated as secrets. This
   benchmark records no such exclusion, and #209 does not invent one. The
   positive stays as authored, and the row is open for a person to decide
   whether this is a product gap or a documented scope exclusion.

The detector-coverage `confluent-cloud-api-secret-prefixed-shape-*` positives
were generated before the checksum was known. They are flat random bodies that
fail the provider checksum, so they now score as retained policy regressions
(must-redact/T1 → policy/T3). Nothing was relabelled to pass.

## Not authored, and why

- **Heroku refresh tokens, client secrets, grant codes and session tokens.**
  They are bare-UUID secrets that no family claims, and a secret is never a
  benign control.
- **The 40-hex `.netrc` example.** It is an undated provider shape. Silence on
  it would turn a hypothesis into a negative expectation.
- **A non-`AA` 65-character value.** Whether `AA` is required is disputed
  (trufflehog#4510, osv-scalibr).
- **Confluent legacy unprefixed secrets and `cflt-scim_` values.** Both are
  secrets. The legacy form belongs to #207.
- **Notion `ntn_`, Anthropic `sk-ant-oat…`/admin keys and Datadog
  `ddpat_`/`ddsat_` as controls.** All are secrets of other classes.
- **Google `AQ.` values.** They rest on unconfirmed community reports.
- **Length, alphabet or suffix twins on tool-only fields** (Anthropic body and
  `AA`, Datadog body, Notion body). No such twin was authored.
